Facts and numbers I assumed

  • A generic Mediterranean-style harbour: a rubble-mound breakwater (stone core, armour blocks on the sea side, a concrete crown wall), a short opposite pier, and quays built from reinforced-concrete cellular caissons. Many real ports use piles or block walls instead.
  • Natural seabed at -10 m, basin dredged to -16 m, a typical depth for large container ships (draughts of about 14–16 m). Real ports range from about 10 to 18 m.
  • Caissons are towed out floating, sunk onto a stone bed by letting water into their cells, then filled with sand and capped. Here the tug and the sinking are shown in simplified form.
  • The dredger is a cutter suction dredger pumping spoil to shore through a floating pipeline.
  • Entrance lights follow IALA Region A (Europe): green on the breakwater head, which is on the right of a ship entering, and red on the pier head on the left.
  • The construction order is shown as survey → breakwater → dredging → caissons → quay equipment. Real projects overlap these phases.
  • The model is schematic, not to scale. Depths below the waterline are drawn at half scale so the cutaway fits the frame.
