Facts and numbers I assumed

  • The port is a generic Mediterranean-style harbour: a rubble-mound breakwater (core of quarry stone, a filter layer, an armour layer of large blocks, and a concrete crown wall), and quays built from reinforced-concrete cellular caissons. Many real ports use piles, sheet piles or block walls instead.
  • -16 m design depth: typical for a port that takes large container ships (draughts of about 14–16 m). Real ports range from about 10 to 18 m.
  • The caissons are cast in a floating dock, towed out, sunk onto a levelled stone bed by letting water into their cells, then filled with sand (sometimes gravel or rubble) and capped with a concrete beam carrying bollards.
  • The dredger is a cutter suction dredger (draga aspirante-refluente con disgregatore) pumping spoil through a floating pipeline.
  • Entrance lights follow IALA Region A (Europe): green on the right and red on the left for a ship entering port.
  • The construction order is shown as studies → breakwater → dredging → quays. Real projects overlap these phases.
  • Depth contours (-5, -10, -15 m) and the cross-section are schematic. Slopes are drawn steeper than real ones (about 1:1.5 to 1:2) so everything fits the frame.
